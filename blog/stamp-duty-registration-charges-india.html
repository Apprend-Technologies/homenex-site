Stamp Duty and Registration Charges in India: State-Wise Guide

Updated September 2024  ·  9 min read

Stamp duty and registration charges are mandatory government fees you pay when buying property in India. Together, they can add 5-10% to your property cost — a significant amount that many first-time buyers underestimate. This guide provides state-wise rates, explains how to calculate them, and highlights exemptions that can save you money.

What Is Stamp Duty?

Stamp duty is a tax levied by the state government on the transfer of property ownership. It is calculated as a percentage of the property's market value or the actual transaction value, whichever is higher. The "market value" is determined by the government's ready reckoner rate (also called circle rate or guideline value, depending on the state).

Stamp duty must be paid before or at the time of executing the sale deed. Without proper stamping, the sale deed is not considered a valid legal document and cannot be presented as evidence in court.

What Are Registration Charges?

Registration charges are a separate fee paid to register the property transaction with the Sub-Registrar's Office. This is governed by the Indian Registration Act, 1908. Registration makes the transfer of ownership official and legally enforceable.

In most states, the registration charge is 1% of the property value, though some states have a flat fee or a capped amount.

How to Calculate Stamp Duty

Stamp duty is calculated on the higher of:

  • Agreement value — The price mentioned in the sale agreement
  • Ready reckoner rate — The government's published minimum rate for the area (also called circle rate or guideline value)

Example Calculation

Suppose you are buying a flat in Pune, Maharashtra:

  • Agreement value: Rs 75,00,000
  • Ready reckoner value: Rs 70,00,000
  • Stamp duty calculated on: Rs 75,00,000 (higher of the two)
  • Stamp duty (male buyer): 6% = Rs 4,50,000
  • Stamp duty (female buyer): 5% = Rs 3,75,000
  • Registration charges: 1% = Rs 30,000 (capped at Rs 30,000 in Maharashtra)
  • Total for male buyer: Rs 4,80,000
  • Total for female buyer: Rs 4,05,000
  • Saving with female buyer: Rs 75,000

How to Pay Stamp Duty

Stamp duty can be paid through several methods:

  1. E-stamping — The most common method. Pay online through the SHCIL (Stock Holding Corporation of India) portal and receive an e-stamp certificate. Most states now support this.
  2. Franking — Pay at an authorised bank or franking centre. The amount is franked (stamped) on the agreement paper.
  3. Non-judicial stamp paper — Purchase stamp paper of the required value from an authorised vendor. This is becoming less common as e-stamping takes over.

Exemptions and Concessions

For Women Buyers

As noted above, registering property in a woman's name provides a stamp duty concession in many states. For joint purchases, the concession typically applies when the woman is the first named buyer.

For Affordable Housing

Some states offer reduced stamp duty for properties below certain value thresholds:

  • Maharashtra — 1% stamp duty for affordable housing (up to 500 sq ft in Mumbai, up to Rs 25 lakh in other areas under PMAY)
  • Karnataka — 2-3% for properties under Rs 20-45 lakh
  • Rajasthan — Concessions available under Mukhyamantri Jan Awas Yojana

For Agricultural Land

Most states have lower stamp duty rates for agricultural land compared to residential or commercial property.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Understating the property value — Paying stamp duty on a value lower than the ready reckoner rate is illegal and can result in penalties, back-taxes, and even criminal charges
  • Paying after the deadline — Stamp duty must be paid before or at the time of registration. Late payment attracts a penalty of 2% per month (up to 200% of the stamp duty in some states)
  • Ignoring the ready reckoner rate — Even if you negotiate a lower purchase price, stamp duty will be charged on the ready reckoner rate if it is higher
  • Not claiming input GST credit — For commercial property purchases, the GST paid can be claimed as input tax credit if you are a registered business

Stamp Duty and Income Tax

Stamp duty and registration charges are eligible for tax deduction under Section 80C of the Income Tax Act, up to a combined limit of Rs 1.5 lakh per year. This deduction is available in the year of purchase — do not forget to claim it.

Final Thoughts

Stamp duty and registration charges are unavoidable costs, but understanding the rates, exemptions, and payment process helps you plan your budget accurately. Always register property through proper legal channels, and consider the women buyer concession to save a significant amount.
