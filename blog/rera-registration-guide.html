RERA Registration: What Every Home Buyer Must Know

Updated September 2024  ·  9 min read

The Real Estate (Regulation and Development) Act, 2016 — commonly known as RERA — was a landmark reform for Indian real estate. Before RERA, home buyers had little legal recourse against delayed projects, misleading advertisements, or builders who diverted funds. RERA changed that by introducing accountability, transparency, and a structured complaint mechanism for the first time.

Whether you are buying your first home or investing in property, understanding RERA is essential to protect your money and your rights.

What Is RERA?

RERA is a central law that regulates the real estate sector across India. It mandates that:

  • Every real estate project with more than 8 units or over 500 sq metres of land must be registered with the state RERA authority before being marketed or sold
  • Builders must deposit 70% of project funds in a separate escrow account, preventing fund diversion to other projects
  • All advertisements must match RERA-registered details — builders cannot claim amenities or features not disclosed in the registration
  • Real estate agents must also be RERA-registered to operate legally

How to Check RERA Registration

Before buying any under-construction property, always verify the RERA registration. Here is how:

  1. Ask the builder or agent for the RERA registration number
  2. Visit your state RERA website (see list below)
  3. Enter the registration number in the project search section
  4. Verify the project details: builder name, project name, approved plans, number of units, completion date, and any complaints filed

Your Rights as a Buyer Under RERA

RERA gives home buyers several important rights:

Right to Information

Builders must disclose the complete project layout, approved plans, carpet area, timeline, and details of all approvals on the RERA website. You have the right to access all of this before making a purchase decision.

Right to Fair Carpet Area

RERA standardised the definition of carpet area as the "net usable floor area" excluding external walls, service shafts, and balconies. Builders can only charge you based on carpet area, not super built-up area. This eliminated the common practice of inflating area calculations.

Right to Timely Possession

If the builder fails to deliver possession by the date promised in the agreement, you are entitled to either:

  • Full refund with interest — at the SBI MCLR rate plus 2%
  • Monthly compensation — for every month of delay until possession

Right to Quality

Builders are liable for structural defects and quality issues for 5 years after possession. If a defect is reported within this period, the builder must fix it within 30 days at no cost to the buyer.

Right Against False Promises

If the final property differs from what was promised in the brochure or agreement — missing amenities, changed layout, reduced area — you can demand compensation or a refund.

How to File a RERA Complaint

If a builder violates RERA provisions, you can file a complaint:

  1. Register on the state RERA portal with your personal details and project information
  2. File the complaint online — describe the issue, upload supporting documents (agreement, receipts, correspondence)
  3. Pay the complaint fee — typically Rs 1,000 to Rs 5,000 depending on the state
  4. Attend hearings — RERA authorities will schedule hearings and the builder must respond
  5. Receive the order — RERA can order refunds, compensation, interest, or penalties on the builder
Pro Tip: RERA complaints are resolved much faster than civil courts — typically within 60 days. You can also appeal to the RERA Appellate Tribunal if unsatisfied with the order.

What RERA Does Not Cover

While RERA is powerful, it has limitations:

  • Resale properties — RERA covers only new project sales from builder to buyer, not secondary market transactions
  • Commercial projects — Coverage varies by state; not all states include commercial real estate
  • Plotted development — Some states exempt plotted developments from RERA registration
  • Ongoing projects — Projects that received completion certificates before RERA came into effect are exempt

Checklist Before Buying an Under-Construction Property

  1. Verify RERA registration number on the state website
  2. Check the registered completion date — is it realistic?
  3. Read the registered agreement carefully — carpet area, amenities, payment schedule
  4. Confirm the builder has not exceeded the registered number of floors or units
  5. Check for any complaints filed against the project on the RERA portal
  6. Ensure the agreement has a penalty clause for delayed possession
  7. Verify that 70% of buyer payments are going into the escrow account

Final Thoughts

RERA has fundamentally changed the power dynamics in Indian real estate. Buyers now have legal backing, transparency, and a fast complaint resolution mechanism. But these protections only work if you use them — always verify RERA registration, read the agreement carefully, and do not hesitate to file a complaint if your rights are violated.
